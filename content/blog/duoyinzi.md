You've been studying 长 as *cháng* (long, length) for weeks. Then someone says *zhǎng* and points at the same character. You're not hearing things — that's 多音字 (*duōyīnzì*), literally "many-sound characters."

These are characters that have two or more distinct pronunciations, each with its own meaning and usage. They're not typos, regional variations, or mistakes. They're a systematic feature of Chinese that every intermediate learner needs to understand.

---

## Why Do They Exist?

The short answer: Chinese has far more meanings than it has syllables. With only around 400 distinct syllables (and around 1,300 including tones), the language has always had to pack multiple meanings into single phonetic units.

多音字 arise from several historical processes:

**Merger of originally distinct words.** Characters that once represented different words with different sounds gradually converged in writing but kept their distinct pronunciations in speech.

**Functional shift.** A character used as a noun developed a verbal use with a different pronunciation — the classic example being 好 *hǎo* (good) vs 好 *hào* (to be fond of).

**Literary vs colloquial readings.** Some characters have one pronunciation in classical contexts and another in everyday speech, common in characters borrowed from Classical Chinese into modern Mandarin.

## The Most Common 多音字

### 长, cháng vs zhǎng

| | Pinyin | Meaning | Example |
|--|--------|---------|---------|
| 长 | cháng | long; length; strong point | 长城 *Chángchéng*, Great Wall |
| 长 | zhǎng | to grow; to increase; chief; elder | 成长 *chéngzhǎng*, to grow up |

> 这条路很**长**。*Zhè tiáo lù hěn cháng.* This road is very long.
> 她**长**得很漂亮。*Tā zhǎng de hěn piàoliang.* She grew up to be very beautiful.

### 好, hǎo vs hào

| | Pinyin | Meaning | Example |
|--|--------|---------|---------|
| 好 | hǎo | good; well; OK | 你好 *nǐ hǎo*, hello |
| 好 | hào | to be fond of; to have a tendency to | 好奇 *hàoqí*, curious |

> 他学习很**好**。*Tā xuéxí hěn hǎo.* He studies well.
> 他**好**奇心很强。*Tā hàoqíxīn hěn qiáng.* He has a very strong sense of curiosity.

### 号, hào vs háo

| | Pinyin | Meaning | Example |
|--|--------|---------|---------|
| 号 | hào | number; day of the month; size; signal | 电话号码 *diànhuà hàomǎ*, phone number |
| 号 | háo | to wail; to howl (literary) | 号哭 *háokū*, to wail and cry |

In everyday use, you'll encounter *hào* almost exclusively. *Háo* is rare and literary.

### 中, zhōng vs zhòng

| | Pinyin | Meaning | Example |
|--|--------|---------|---------|
| 中 | zhōng | middle; center; China | 中国 *Zhōngguó*, China |
| 中 | zhòng | to hit (a target); to be hit by | 中奖 *zhòngjiǎng*, to win a prize |

> 他住在**中**国。*Tā zhù zài Zhōngguó.* He lives in China.
> 他**中**了头奖。*Tā zhòng le tóujiǎng.* He won the jackpot.

### 行, xíng vs háng

| | Pinyin | Meaning | Example |
|--|--------|---------|---------|
| 行 | xíng | to walk; OK; capable; will do | 行！*Xíng!*, OK! / Sure! |
| 行 | háng | row; line; profession; business | 银行 *yínháng*, bank |

> **行**，没问题。*Xíng, méi wèntí.* Sure, no problem.
> 他在银**行**工作。*Tā zài yínháng gōngzuò.* He works at a bank.

### 还, hái vs huán

| | Pinyin | Meaning | Example |
|--|--------|---------|---------|
| 还 | hái | still; also; even more | 我还没吃。*Wǒ hái méi chī.* I haven't eaten yet. |
| 还 | huán | to return; to give back | 还书 *huán shū*, to return a book |

> 你**还**在学中文吗？*Nǐ hái zài xué zhōngwén ma?* Are you still learning Chinese?
> 我要**还**这本书。*Wǒ yào huán zhè běn shū.* I need to return this book.

### 得, de vs dé vs děi

This one has three pronunciations, all extremely common:

| | Pinyin | Function | Example |
|--|--------|---------|---------|
| 得 | de | structural particle (after verb, before complement) | 说得很好 *shuō de hěn hǎo*, speaks very well |
| 得 | dé | to get; to obtain | 得到 *dédào*, to obtain |
| 得 | děi | must; have to (spoken) | 我得走了。*Wǒ děi zǒu le.* I have to go. |

> 他跑**得**很快。*Tā pǎo de hěn kuài.* He runs very fast.
> 你**得**努力学习。*Nǐ děi nǔlì xuéxí.* You have to study hard.

### 发, fā vs fà

| | Pinyin | Meaning | Example |
|--|--------|---------|---------|
| 发 | fā | to send; to develop; to emit | 发现 *fāxiàn*, to discover |
| 发 | fà | hair (on the head) | 头发 *tóufa*, hair |

> 我给你**发**消息。*Wǒ gěi nǐ fā xiāoxi.* I'll send you a message.
> 她的头**发**很长。*Tā de tóufa hěn cháng.* Her hair is very long.

### 地, dì vs de

| | Pinyin | Function | Example |
|--|--------|---------|---------|
| 地 | dì | earth; ground; place | 地图 *dìtú*, map |
| 地 | de | structural particle (before verb) | 慢慢地走 *mànmàn de zǒu*, walk slowly |

> 这块**地**很大。*Zhè kuài dì hěn dà.* This piece of land is very large.
> 他认真**地**学习。*Tā rènzhēn de xuéxí.* He studies seriously.

## How to Know Which Pronunciation to Use

There's no shortcut — context and vocabulary are the only reliable guides. But a few patterns help:

**Compound words are your best hint.** If you know the word 银行 *yínháng* (bank), you know that 行 in this context is *háng*. Learning characters through words rather than in isolation builds this naturally.

**Grammar function helps.** Structural particles like 得 *de* and 地 *de* appear in fixed grammatical positions — once you know the grammar, you know the pronunciation.

**Frequency matters.** For most 多音字, one pronunciation is far more common than the other. 号 is almost always *hào*; *háo* is rare. When in doubt, the more common reading is usually right.

**Listen actively.** Native speakers never hesitate on these — the pronunciation is automatic because it's tied to meaning, not just the character's shape. Listening practice builds the same automatic responses over time.
