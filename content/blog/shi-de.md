You already know 是 means "is/are/am" and 的 marks possession or modifies nouns. But together, sandwiching a verb phrase, they do something completely different: they emphasize circumstantial details about a past event.

> 我**是**昨天买**的**这本书。*Wǒ shì zuótiān mǎi de zhè běn shū.* — I bought this book **yesterday** (not some other time).

This is the 是…的 structure, and it's one of the most useful grammatical patterns in Mandarin.

---

## What It Does

是…的 doesn't describe what happened. It focuses attention on *how* or *when* or *where* or *by whom* something already known to have happened took place.

The thing that happened is assumed or understood. What's new information — what 是…的 is highlighting — is the circumstantial detail.

Compare these two sentences:

> 他买了一本书。*Tā mǎi le yī běn shū.* — He bought a book. *(new information: the event itself)*

> 他**是**在书店买**的**。*Tā shì zài shūdiàn mǎi de.* — He bought it **at the bookstore**. *(new information: where)*

The second sentence assumes you already know he bought a book. 是…的 tells you *where*.

---

## The Structure

The basic pattern:

> **是** + [emphasised element] + verb + **的**

The emphasised element — time, place, manner, agent — comes right after 是:

| What you emphasise | Example |
|---|---|
| **When** | 是昨天来的 *shì zuótiān lái de* — came **yesterday** |
| **Where** | 是在上海出生的 *shì zài Shànghǎi chūshēng de* — was born **in Shanghai** |
| **How / by what means** | 是坐飞机去的 *shì zuò fēijī qù de* — went **by plane** |
| **With whom** | 是和朋友一起去的 *shì hé péngyǒu yīqǐ qù de* — went **with a friend** |
| **By whom (passive)** | 是他做的 *shì tā zuò de* — was done **by him** |

---

## Where the Object Goes

With a direct object, you have two options:

> 我**是**在北京买**的**这本书。 *Wǒ shì zài Běijīng mǎi de zhè běn shū.*
> 我**是**在北京买这本书**的**。 *Wǒ shì zài Běijīng mǎi zhè běn shū de.*

Both mean "I bought this book in Beijing." The difference is minor: in the first, 的 comes before the object; in the second, after. Both are grammatically fine, but putting 的 before the object (first version) is often felt to be more natural in spoken Chinese.

---

## Dropping 是

In spoken Chinese, 是 is often dropped entirely. The 的 at the end is what signals the structure:

> 你**是**什么时候来**的**？ → 你什么时候来**的**？

Both mean "When did you arrive?" The second is more colloquial. You'll hear the short form constantly in conversation.

Be careful: without 是, the sentence looks like a plain statement with a nominalizing 的. Context tells you which it is — if the verb refers to a completed past action and the sentence ends with 的, it's almost certainly 是…的.

---

## Only for Past Events

是…的 is used only with actions that have already occurred. Using it with future or ongoing actions is a mistake:

> ✓ 你**是**怎么来**的**？*Nǐ shì zěnme lái de?* — How did you get here? (asking about a trip that's already happened)
> ✗ 你明天**是**怎么来**的**？ — Wrong if the trip hasn't happened yet; use 你明天打算怎么来？*Nǐ míngtiān dǎsuàn zěnme lái?* instead

If the event hasn't happened yet, you can't use 是…的 to ask about the circumstances.

---

## Common Mistakes

**Using 是…的 when the event itself is new information**

> ✗ 他**是**昨天买了一本书**的**。

If you're telling someone that the buying happened (new info), use a plain sentence with 了. Reserve 是…的 for when the event is already known and you're highlighting a detail about it.

**Forgetting 的 at the end**

> ✗ 我是昨天来。

Without 的, this is ungrammatical. The structure requires 的 — or at least the sentence must end with 的 even when 是 is dropped.

---

## Asking Questions With 是…的

是…的 questions are formed by adding 吗 at the end, or using a question word inside the structure:

> 你是昨天来的吗？*Nǐ shì zuótiān lái de ma?* — Did you arrive yesterday?
> 你是几点来的？*Nǐ shì jǐ diǎn lái de?* — What time did you arrive?
> 你是怎么来的？*Nǐ shì zěnme lái de?* — How did you get here?
> 你是跟谁来的？*Nǐ shì gēn shéi lái de?* — Who did you come with?

These are extremely common questions in everyday conversation — far more natural than the longer alternatives.

---

## A Quick Reference

| Pattern | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| 是 + time + verb + 的 | When |
| 是 + 在 + place + verb + 的 | Where |
| 是 + 怎么/坐… + verb + 的 | How |
| 是 + 和/跟 + person + verb + 的 | With whom |
| 是 + person + verb + 的 | By whom |

Once you start listening for this structure, you'll hear it everywhere — it's one of the most natural ways to ask for and give background details in Mandarin.
