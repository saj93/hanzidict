If you've been researching the HSK recently and found conflicting information — different word counts, different level structures, different exam formats — you're not confused. The system has changed multiple times since 2021, and most resources online are out of date.

This article covers the current HSK system as of 2026, what changed from the old system, and what it actually means for your learning.

---

## A Brief History: Three Versions of the HSK

**HSK 2.0 (2010–2021)** — the system most learners know. Six levels (HSK 1–6), with HSK 1 covering 150 words and HSK 6 requiring roughly 5,000. A pass at HSK 4 was the standard benchmark for working or studying in China.

**HSK 3.0, 2021 version** — released by the Ministry of Education in March 2021 and implemented from July 2021. Six levels became nine. Vocabulary requirements more than doubled: the new system requires 11,092 words across all nine levels, compared to roughly 5,000 in the old system.

**HSK 3.0, 2026 version** — released in November 2025 and effective from July 2026, this is a significant revision of the 2021 standard. The most notable change: it returns to an "inverted pyramid" structure, reducing vocabulary requirements for levels 1–5 while concentrating more words at level 6.

If you're preparing for the HSK right now, the 2026 version applies.

---

## The New Structure: 9 Levels, 3 Stages

HSK 3.0 expands from 6 levels to 9, organised into three stages: Elementary (Levels 1–3), Intermediate (Levels 4–6), and Advanced (Levels 7–9).

Here's what each level requires in the 2026 system:

**Elementary Stage**

Level 1: 300 words, listening + reading only, CEFR A1 — basic greetings, simple questions, survival phrases.

Level 2: 500 words total (200 new), listening + reading only, CEFR A1+ — simple conversations about daily life and basic needs.

Level 3: 1,000 words total (500 new), listening + reading + speaking, CEFR A2 — first speaking requirement. Discuss familiar topics, express simple opinions, handle basic social situations.

**Intermediate Stage**

Level 4: 2,000 words total (1,000 new) — intermediate conversations, expressing opinions, handling most everyday situations.

Levels 5–6 continue toward fluency, with HSK 6 being the practical threshold for working and studying in China.

**Advanced Stage**

Levels 7–9 are designed for learners pursuing Chinese-language university degrees, professional translation, Chinese studies, international policy, or cross-border work. There is one exam for this tier; the band you receive (7, 8, or 9) is determined by your score. HSK 7–9 covers approximately 5,636 words on its own.

## What Actually Changed From the Old System

**More levels, more precision.** The most meaningful expansion happened between old HSK 5 and HSK 6. The new system adds two levels in that gap, giving learners a clearer picture of where they stand in the intermediate-to-advanced range.

**Speaking added at higher levels.** The old HSK was written-only. HSK 7–9 now includes a spoken component, meaning advanced certification requires producing the language, not just reading and writing it.

**Handwriting requirements.** There is now one set of characters that candidates must recognise and another they must be able to write. Writing requirements are progressive: lower levels focus on recognition, while writing becomes increasingly important from the intermediate stage onward.

**Cultural content.** Chinese cultural knowledge — history, idioms, customs — is woven through the curriculum at all levels.

---

## If Your Level Dropped on Paper

Many learners who mapped their old HSK level to the new system found themselves at a "lower" level. That is not a setback — it's the system giving you a more accurate picture. Your ability is unchanged; what changed is the measurement. The new system is simply more demanding at each step, particularly in the intermediate range.

## What This Means for Learning (Not Just Exams)

Most learners will never take the HSK, and that's fine. The vocabulary lists are still useful as a structured learning framework even without exam plans.

A few practical points:

**Check which version your resources use.** Most Anki decks, textbooks, and apps were built on the old HSK 2.0 system. If your deck says "HSK 4, 600 words," it's using the old system. The new HSK 4 requires 2,000 cumulative words.

**The levels now align with CEFR.** This makes it easier to compare with other language certifications. HSK 3 ≈ A2, HSK 6 ≈ B2/C1.
