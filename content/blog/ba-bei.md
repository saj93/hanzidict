Chinese sentence order is usually Subject – Verb – Object (SVO), just like English. But two patterns change that in useful ways:

**把** (*bǎ*) moves the object before the verb, emphasising the effect on the object.
**被** (*bèi*) introduces who performed an action on the subject — a passive construction.

Both are important at the intermediate level, and both are frequently misused because they look simpler than they are.

---

## 把: Putting the Object First

The 把 structure takes an object and places it before the verb:

> 我吃了苹果。*Wǒ chī le píngguǒ.* — I ate the apple. *(normal SVO)*
> 我**把**苹果吃了。*Wǒ bǎ píngguǒ chī le.* — I ate the apple (up). *(把 structure)*

Both sentences have the same basic meaning, but the second emphasises that the apple was dealt with — finished off, fully acted upon. 把 sentences highlight the result or disposal of the object.

### The Pattern

> **Subject + 把 + Object + Verb + Result/Complement**

The verb in a 把 sentence almost always needs something after it — a result complement, directional complement, or 了 — to indicate what happened to the object:

> 他**把**作业写完了。*Tā bǎ zuòyè xiě wán le.* — He finished writing his homework.
> 她**把**书放在桌子上。*Tā bǎ shū fàng zài zhuōzi shàng.* — She put the book on the table.
> 我**把**那封信翻译成中文了。*Wǒ bǎ nà fēng xìn fānyì chéng zhōngwén le.* — I translated that letter into Chinese.

### When to Use 把

把 is appropriate when:

- The verb expresses a **deliberate action** that affects the object
- The result of the action on the object is the focus
- The object is **definite** (a specific thing, not "a book" but "the book" or "my book")

> 我**把**那本书借给他了。*Wǒ bǎ nà běn shū jiè gěi tā le.* — I lent him that book.
> 她**把**房间打扫得很干净。*Tā bǎ fángjiān dǎsǎo de hěn gānjìng.* — She cleaned the room (so it was very clean).

### When Not to Use 把

- With psychological or mental verbs (知道, 喜欢, 觉得 — you can't 把 something you know or like)
- When the object is indefinite ("a book" in general)
- When the verb has no result or complement (a bare verb with no outcome)
- In passive constructions (use 被 instead)

> ✗ 我把他喜欢。*Wǒ bǎ tā xǐhuān.*
> ✓ 我喜欢他。*Wǒ xǐhuān tā.* — I like him.

## 被: The Passive

被 introduces the agent — who performed the action — in a passive sentence. The subject is the one being acted upon:

> 他的钱包**被**小偷偷了。*Tā de qiánbāo bèi xiǎotōu tōu le.* — His wallet was stolen by a thief.
> 那个窗户**被**风吹坏了。*Nà gè chuānghù bèi fēng chuī huài le.* — That window was broken by the wind.

### The Pattern

> **Subject + 被 + Agent + Verb + Complement**

The agent after 被 can be omitted when it's unknown or irrelevant:

> 他的车**被**撞了。*Tā de chē bèi zhuàng le.* — His car was hit. *(by someone/something unspecified)*
> 作业**被**交了。*Zuòyè bèi jiāo le.* — The homework was handed in.

### The Tone of 被

In Chinese, 被 sentences typically carry a **negative or unwanted connotation** — something unpleasant happened to the subject. This is different from English passive, which is neutral:

> 我**被**老板批评了。*Wǒ bèi lǎobǎn pīpíng le.* — I was criticized by my boss. *(unpleasant)*
> 那个罪犯**被**警察抓住了。*Nà gè zuìfàn bèi jǐngchá zhuā zhù le.* — The criminal was caught by the police. *(bad for the criminal)*

Saying 我被提升了 (*Wǒ bèi tíshēng le* — I was promoted) sounds odd to native speakers because promotion is positive and 被 implies something was done *to* you that you didn't want. For positive passive situations, Chinese usually restructures to active voice.

## Side by Side

| | 把 | 被 |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | What was done to the object | Who did the action to the subject |
| Object position | After 把, before verb | Subject of sentence |
| Typical connotation | Neutral (disposal/result) | Negative (unwanted) |
| Agent | Subject of sentence | After 被 |

## Common Mistakes

**Bare verb in 把 sentences**

> ✗ 我把书看。
> ✓ 我把书看完了。*Wǒ bǎ shū kàn wán le.* — I finished reading the book.

The verb needs a complement. Without one, the sentence is grammatically incomplete.

**Using 被 for positive events**

> ✗ 她被邀请了，很开心。(awkward — being invited is positive)
> ✓ 她收到了邀请，很开心。*Tā shōu dào le yāoqǐng, hěn kāixīn.* — She received an invitation and was happy.

**Indefinite object with 把**

> ✗ 我把一本书看完了。*Wǒ bǎ yī běn shū kàn wán le.*
> ✓ 我把那本书看完了。*Wǒ bǎ nà běn shū kàn wán le.* — I finished reading that book.

把 requires a specific, definite object.

## Practice With Real Sentences

> 请**把**门关上。*Qǐng bǎ mén guān shàng.* — Please close the door.
> 我**把**手机忘在家里了。*Wǒ bǎ shǒujī wàng zài jiā lǐ le.* — I left my phone at home.
> 他的自行车**被**偷了。*Tā de zìxíngchē bèi tōu le.* — His bicycle was stolen.
> 那个消息**被**他们知道了。*Nà gè xiāoxi bèi tāmen zhīdào le.* — That news became known to them.

把 and 被 are worth practising together because they're both about foregrounding something that standard SVO order buries. Once the structures become natural, your sentences will sound considerably more fluent.
